The way we travel
04
01

Arrive in the clouds

Fly into Paro, one of the world's most dramatic landings, and step into a valley of rice terraces, prayer flags and mountain air. Your guide is waiting — and so is the slowest, most welcoming pace you have ever travelled at.

02

Climb to the sacred

A morning hike through pine and prayer flags leads to Tiger's Nest, a monastery clinging to a cliff 900 metres above the valley. The reward is quiet, wide and unforgettable.

03

Sit with silence

Share butter tea with monks, light a lamp at a hilltop temple and let the rhythm of horns and chanting reset your own. Bhutan does not hurry — and neither will you.

04

Celebrate with the valley

Time your journey with a Tshechu and watch masked dancers whirl through a dzong courtyard as families in their finest gho and kira cheer on. It is the Bhutan that no postcard captures.

When to go

Four seasons, four Bhutans

March — May

Rhododendrons in bloom

Valleys flush with colour and the great festivals begin — Paro Tshechu, Thimphu's prayer-flag hillsides, rhododendron forests above Dochula. Warm days, clear mountain views; the most loved time to visit.

  • Paro Tshechu festival
  • Rhododendron blossom
  • Tiger's Nest in perfect light
June — August

Emerald quiet

The monsoon paints Bhutan in every shade of green. Afternoon showers, mist in the valleys and far fewer travellers — a slow season for culture, cuisine and photography.

  • Lush green valleys
  • Fewer visitors
  • Excellent value
September — November

Crystal skies

Crisp, dry, golden: the clearest Himalayan views of the year. Rice terraces turn to gold, Thimphu Tshechu fills the dzong, and black-necked cranes begin arriving in Phobjikha.

  • Thimphu Tshechu
  • Clearest mountain views
  • Black-necked cranes return
December — February

Snow & cranes

Cold, bright, uncrowded. Snow-dusted passes, steaming hot-stone baths and cranes gliding over Phobjikha. Monasteries are quiet, mornings are magical, and hotels are at their best value.

  • Snow-dusted Himalaya
  • Cranes in Phobjikha
  • Quiet monasteries

Entry permit & fee essentials for Indian travellers

Entry permit

No visa needed. Carry a valid passport (6 months) or voter ID; we arrange the permit.

02

SDF

₹1,200 per person per night (children 6–12 half, under 6 free).

All FAQs

It depends on your passport. International visitors need a visa and must have it approved before they travel. Indian travellers do not need a visa but they need an entry permit. Visitors from Bangladesh and the Maldives can also get their visa on arrival.

Send us your passport details by email or WhatsApp. We arrange the visa or permit through our partners in Bhutan. International travellers pay the visa fee and the SDF during the application. Indian travellers pay only the SDF. Indian permits can also be processed on arrival but applying before you travel avoids waiting at the border or airport.

You need three things. A digital copy of your passport that stays valid for at least six months after you leave Bhutan. A recent passport size photograph. Valid travel insurance for the full length of your trip. Indian travellers need different documents for the entry permit and they are listed in FAQ 6.

International travellers pay USD 40 per person for the visa. It is a one time fee and it is not refundable. Indian travellers pay no fee for the entry permit. Every traveller pays the Sustainable Development Fee on top of this.

It may take up to five working days to process a correctly input visa application.

A correct visa application for international travellers can take up to five working days. Indian permits are usually quicker. We suggest applying at least two weeks before travel so your flights and hotels can be confirmed without stress.
